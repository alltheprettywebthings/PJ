lede: Workwrights is built around five agents. Think of them as your own hiring team, there to sort the quality listings from the riff-raff.

Use an agent's name and your request: "Scout, take a look at this job listing." If you're not sure which agent would do the best job, just ask.

| Agent | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| 🐶 [Scout]({{ '/agents/scout/' | relative_url }}) | Finds jobs and does a first inspection, so you can see which are worth your time. |
| 🤔 [Evaluator]({{ '/agents/evaluator/' | relative_url }}) | Runs strict checks on jobs, your resume and your evidence bank, and finds the gaps. |
| 🖋️ [Editor]({{ '/agents/editor/' | relative_url }}) | Suggests edits to your resume and cover letters. Doesn't write for you. |
| 👩‍🔬 [Researcher]({{ '/agents/researcher/' | relative_url }}) | Researches companies, roles, controversies and pay ranges. |
| 🛡️ [Ellis]({{ '/agents/ellis/' | relative_url }}) | A caring career coach for when the search wears you down. |

## Use them in sequence

Names make it easy to hand work from one agent to the next:

1. "Scout, take a look at this job listing."
2. "Evaluator, give me a gap analysis on that listing."
3. "Editor, suggest ways I can address those gaps."

You can also ask any chat in your project to perform any task, and it will call the right tool. I prefer keeping the tasks separate; Workwrights is workflow-agnostic.
