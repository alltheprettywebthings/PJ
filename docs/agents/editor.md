Editor is **not designed to write for you**. It can draft, but you make the final edits, so your resume and cover letters keep your voice. That's also how you keep an AI from putting things on your resume that aren't true.

## Choose how Editor suggests edits

The first time you ask for suggested edits, Editor asks two quick questions (added in v0.9):

- **Suggested changes or pre-written sentences.** With suggested changes, Editor tells you what to change and why, and you write it. With pre-written sentences, Editor drafts wording from your evidence bank for you to reread and rewrite. Either way, it uses only claims in your evidence bank and follows your voice profile.
- **Relevance or resume order.** By relevance, the edits that matter most to the job come first. In resume order, Editor goes from the top of your resume to the bottom, and still marks each edit's priority.

Editor remembers your answers. Change them any time:

> Switch to pre-written sentences.

> List edits in resume order.

You can also ask for something different for one request: "Give me pre-written sentences for this one."

## How edits are ranked

Each suggestion names the job requirement it addresses and gets a priority: **High** for a stated requirement or one the posting stresses, **Medium** for a preferred qualification or supporting theme, and **Low** for a nice-to-have or a style change. Edits that need something your evidence bank doesn't have yet are listed last, under "Needs evidence first", with the question that would unlock each one. Editor never writes the missing claim for you.

## What to ask for

- Advice on specific resume bullet points.
- Ways to tailor a cover letter to a job's requirements.
- A check on whether a draft matches your usual voice and tone.
